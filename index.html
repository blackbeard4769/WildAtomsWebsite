Studio Thesis

We build games from the ground floor up, not the top down.

Wild Atoms Software Division is a small team making tightly-scoped, mechanically honest survival games. No bloat, no filler systems — every mechanic earns its place before it ships.

Our first title, Pre-Echo, is currently in pre-production: a third-person tactical survival-horror built in Unreal Engine 5.

THIRD-PERSON TACTICAL SURVIVAL-HORROR PC / CONSOLES UNREAL ENGINE 5

Survival meets Dead Space, grounded in the boots of a firefighter.

01 — The Logline

You are the first response to a disaster that hasn't happened yet.

A routine call to a secretive subterranean research facility ends in a catastrophic interdimensional breach. Two elite firemen — armed with no guns, only rescue tools and instinct — must climb eight stories out of a death trap.

They'll hunt time-warping anomalies, forge weapons from otherworldly remains, and break the facility's own lockdown to stop a global catastrophe.

02 — The Hook

Firefighters respond after disaster strikes. Pre-Echo flips that.

In this facility, sound travels faster than light. You will hear your own future screams as a warning — response and anticipation, collapsed into one dread-soaked mechanic.

Dead Space

The setting and dread — a claustrophobic, industrial hull with nowhere safe to stand.

Bloodborne

Brutal, tactical melee. No guns — just tools and timing.

Prey

Environmental problem-solving over brute force. The facility is the puzzle.

03 — The Setting

The Subterranean Collider

A privatized, off-the-books Deep-Earth Particle Collider, buried beneath the industrial sectors. An experimental Tachyon Condensate ruptured — a glowing puddle of liquid time — and tore a sinkhole into a dimension where time runs backward.

The facility's automated lockdown treats the breach like a rigid firewall: hard-coded to deny all outbound anomalous traffic while logging every incursion below. The only way out is up — through the core, floor by floor, each one a new failure of physics.

04 — The Core Loop

Players don't loot ammo. They harvest and adapt.

Survive

Traverse warped environments — zero-gravity flooded basements, inverted chemical fires — with standard fireman traversal tools: grapples, thermal imaging.

Hunt & Parry

Melee Tachyon anomalies. Parrying with a Halligan bar briefly disrupts an enemy's time-stream, opening a lethal counter.

Forge

Extract Tachyon Shards from fallen enemies. Fuse alien tech with firefighting gear at welding stations — a Chrono-Axe that strikes a split-second into the future.

05 — The Time-Burn Mechanic

No health bars. Just borrowed time.

Taking a hit doesn't just cost health — it manipulates your local time. Movement, dodges, and swings slow by half for several seconds. Panic-mashing while slowed gets you killed.

Heal by finding heat sources — raging fires, electrical sparks — to re-anchor your timeline. Oxygen is finite, and every tank spent rescuing a trapped scientist is a tank you don't have for the next floor.

06 — Immersive UI

The visor is the interface. There is no other menu.

Minimalist, diegetic HUD: health reads as Time-Burn glitch textures on the fireman's own body, oxygen lives on the tank gauge, and every readout flickers in Tachyon blue — a constant reminder of how loose your grip on "now" really is.

07 — The Bestiary

Not zombies. Physics experiments gone wrong.

BRUISER

The Pendulums

Monolithic constructs of architectural debris and twisted steel, held together by searing thermal energy. Slow, predictable, devastating heavy hitters.

RANGED

The Reverts

Floating audio-disruptors that swarm and attack with sound waves rather than gas or projectiles — built to disable, not just damage.

08 — The Climax

The Prime Anomaly

The final "boss" isn't a villain — he's the Lead Scientist who fused himself with the mainframe to hold the lockdown and save the surface world. Tachyon radiation bombarded him, merging his body with the console and fracturing him across infinite timelines.

To escape, the firemen must forcefully disconnect him — killing the only man acting as humanity's firewall against the apocalypse. Did we just doom the world by doing our job?

The Field Manual

The Arsenal: Weapons of Life, Reforged

You don't loot ammo — you harvest interdimensional components. Fallen enemies leave behind glowing Tachyon Shards, integrated into standard fireman tools at industrial welding stations.

The Chrono-Axe

The starting fire axe, fused with Tachyon crystals so the blade sits always slightly "in the future." Swing it, and it lands before you've finished the motion.

Stasis Extinguisher

A standard extinguisher loaded with Tachyon crystals. It doesn't put out fires — it freezes a target in a bubble of slow-time for 10 seconds.

The Forge

Every upgrade happens in-world at a welding station. No menus — progression is something you physically build.

Audio Design

Silence Is a Weapon

The game's namesake mechanic: spatial audio lets you hear the screams of anomalies — or your own death — roughly 30 seconds before it happens. You map the threat with your ears long before you ever see it.

It turns every quiet corridor into a countdown, and rewards players who stop moving long enough to listen.

Combat Reads

Reading the Stutter

Because Tachyon creatures operate on their own broken clock, their attacks "stutter" — players must watch for a blue flash of Cherenkov radiation to know exactly when a strike will actually land.

Outside of combat, a Thermal Camera reveals footprints and lets you line up a stealth approach — sneaking in to smash the glowing Tachyon crystals on an enemy's body before it ever wakes up.

Resource Management

The Survival Clock

Oxygen is the primary resource, and it's finite. The tank gauge is built into the character model itself — never a floating menu — and a Time-Burn hit can rewind your remaining air supply, turning a manageable situation into an immediate panic.

Every floor asks the same question: spend your limited O2 and gear saving a trapped scientist — unlocking lore and bypass codes — or hoard it to survive the climb yourself.
